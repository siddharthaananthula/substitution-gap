# The Substitution Gap

**Status: in progress.** Not deployed yet. This README describes what is being built, and it is updated as each piece lands.

Ranking grocery substitutions by the chance the customer accepts them, not by how similar the products look.

---

## The problem

When an online grocery order has an item out of stock, someone chooses a replacement in seconds. Most substitution logic ranks candidates by product similarity: same category, same brand, similar name.

Customers don't decide that way. They seem to accept a different brand of the same thing far more readily than the same brand of a different thing. A rejected substitute usually becomes a refund, which is the most expensive outcome. It loses the sale, and it may make the customer less likely to order again.

**The gap:** similarity and acceptance are different objectives, and they disagree.

## What this project does

1. **Candidate generation from behaviour, not attributes.** Two products count as substitutes when the same shopper buys each of them on different trips but rarely buys both in one basket. This is aggregated across shoppers, with a minimum evidence threshold.
2. **Acceptance labels from revealed behaviour.** When a shopper's usual product is missing from an order and a product from its substitute set appears instead, that counts as evidence of acceptance. See DECISIONS.md, D002, for why the labels are not generated by a rule-based simulator.
3. **Two rankers compared head to head:** a similarity baseline and an acceptance model.
4. **Hard blocks** on flavour and dietary swaps, for example gluten free to regular or lactose free to full dairy.
5. **Customer tolerance** inferred from each shopper's own history.
6. **A cost view.** The difference between the two rankers is expressed in dollars, using stated assumptions for prices and refund costs.

## Data

Instacart Market Basket Analysis, via the [psparks Kaggle mirror](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/psparks/instacart-market-basket-analysis). It has six files: orders, prior and train order lines, products, aisles and departments.

It does **not** contain prices, stock levels, out-of-stock events, substitution outcomes or structured product attributes. Everything built on top of it is documented as an assumption in DECISIONS.md. No employer or proprietary data is used anywhere in this project.

## Evaluation

- Ranking quality on held-out orders: hit rate@k and NDCG@k, comparing similarity against acceptance
- Rate of blocked attribute violations
- Estimated refund cost under each ranker, with sensitivity to the price and cost assumptions

## Stack (planned)

Python 3.11, pandas, Postgres, dbt, scikit-learn / LightGBM (learning to rank), FastAPI, Docker, GitHub Actions, Azure container deployment.

## Known limitations

- Acceptance labels are a behavioural proxy, not observed accept/reject decisions.
- Out-of-stock events are simulated.
- Prices and costs are assumed, so dollar figures are illustrative.
- Product attributes are parsed from product names and are incomplete.
